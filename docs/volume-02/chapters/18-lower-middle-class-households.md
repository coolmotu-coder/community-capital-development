## 18.1 Introduction

Lower-Middle-Class Households occupy the category just above the poorest households in the capital endowment spectrum. These households typically possess a more diversified set of capital assets than Poor Households but remain vulnerable to economic shocks and face constrained mobility pathways.

## 18.2 Defining Characteristics

Lower-Middle-Class Households are characterized by:

- Modest but relatively stable financial capital
- Basic physical assets and adequate, though limited, housing
- Moderate educational attainment with ongoing skill development
- Expanding social networks beyond survival-oriented ties
- Developing relationships with formal institutions

## 18.4 Vulnerability and Resilience

Lower-Middle-Class Households face a distinct vulnerability profile. While they possess more capital than Poor Households, they typically lack the surplus required for substantial investments or the buffer to withstand significant shocks. This makes the management of risk a central concern for this category.

## 18.5 Mobility Pathways

Key mobility pathways for Lower-Middle-Class Households include:

- Educational advancement of subsequent generations
- Gradual asset accumulation through savings
- Expansion of productive skills and labor market opportunities
- Deepening of social networks into higher-capital categories
