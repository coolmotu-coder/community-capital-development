## 22.1 Introduction

Elite Households occupy the uppermost tier of the household capital hierarchy in the Community Capital Development Framework. These households possess substantial capital endowments across all dimensions, exhibit significant power and influence within their communities and broader institutions, and exercise substantial control over the allocation of resources.

## 22.2 Defining Characteristics

Elite Households are characterized by:

- Exceptional financial capital with diversified, often multi-generational wealth
- Extensive physical asset holdings across sectors
- Advanced and often elite educational qualifications
- Dense, high-status social networks with translocal connections
- Deep institutional entanglements, including governance roles

## 22.4 Intergenerational Reproduction

Elite Households typically exhibit high levels of intergenerational capital transmission. Mechanisms include selective educational investment, strategic marriage alliances, direct wealth transfer, institutional mentorship, and the reproduction of social and cultural capital across generations.

## 22.5 Power and Influence

Beyond the accumulation of economic resources, Elite Households exercise influence through:

- Governance and decision-making positions
- Control over employment and economic opportunity
- Shaping of policy agendas and public discourse
- Network-based influence on institutional behavior
