# Questionnaires

> **`[Proposed]`** This section presents proposed questionnaire instruments for the Community Capital Development Project. All questionnaires are original frameworks developed by Anil Kumar Srivastava and have not been independently validated.

---

## 1. Introduction

This section presents the questionnaire instruments developed for the Community Capital Development Project (CCDP). These questionnaires operationalise the assessment tools described in the previous section into detailed survey schedules that can be administered by trained enumerators in the field.

The questionnaires are designed to collect data on household and community capital endowments across five dimensions — financial, physical, human, social, and institutional capital — using a combination of structured questions (for quantitative analysis) and semi-structured questions (for qualitative insight). They draw on established survey instruments from the National Family Health Survey (NFHS), the National Sample Survey Office (NSSO), the Annual Status of Education Report (ASER), and the World Bank's Social Capital Assessment Tool (SCAT).

The questionnaires are intended for use by:
- Government agencies implementing development programmes
- Non-governmental organisations working in rural and urban communities
- Academic researchers conducting community-level assessments
- Community-based organisations seeking to profile their own capital endowments

The questionnaires are not intended as official government instruments. They are original frameworks developed for the CCDP and should be adapted to local contexts before deployment.

## 5. Qualitative Companion Interview Schedule

The Qualitative Companion Interview Schedule (QCIS) is designed to be administered alongside the Core Household Capital Survey to capture qualitative insights that cannot be measured through structured questions. It includes semi-structured interview guides for household heads, focus group discussion guides, and key informant interview guides.

### 5.1 Household Head Semi-Structured Interview

**Topic 1: Household Development Trajectory**
- How has your household's situation changed over the past 10 years?
- What have been the most important changes (positive and negative)?
- What factors contributed to these changes?
- What are your hopes and concerns for the next 10 years?

**Topic 2: Capital Accumulation Strategies**
- How does your household manage financial resources (savings, spending, borrowing)?
- What strategies do you use to build assets (land, housing, education, social connections)?
- What challenges do you face in accumulating capital?
- How do you cope with shocks (health emergencies, crop failure, job loss)?

**Topic 3: Social Networks and Support**
- Who do you turn to for help in times of need?
- How do you maintain relationships with relatives, neighbours, and community members?
- What role do caste, religion, and gender play in your social networks?
- Have your social networks changed over time? How?

**Topic 4: Institutional Engagement**
- How do you interact with government officials and institutions?
- What has been your experience with government programmes?
- What barriers do you face in accessing government services?
- How do you feel about your ability to influence decisions that affect your household?

**Topic 5: Education and Health**
- What are your priorities for your children's education?
- What challenges do you face in accessing healthcare?
- How do you balance health needs with financial constraints?

### 5.2 Focus Group Discussion Guide

**Topic 1: Community Assets and Resources**
- What are the most important assets in our community?
- How are these assets distributed among different groups?
- What assets are missing or inadequate?

**Topic 2: Community Challenges**
- What are the biggest challenges facing our community?
- How do these challenges affect different groups (women, youth, elderly, marginalised communities)?
- What has been done to address these challenges?

**Topic 3: Collective Action**
- Has the community ever come together to address a common problem?
- What were the outcomes?
- What factors enabled or hindered collective action?

**Topic 4: Future Vision**
- What kind of community do you want to see in the next 10 years?
- What needs to change to achieve this vision?
- What role should different groups play in achieving this vision?

### 5.3 Key Informant Interview Guide

**Topic 1: Community Capital Profile**
- How would you describe the capital endowments of this community?
- Which forms of capital are strongest? Which are weakest?
- How have capital endowments changed over time?

**Topic 2: Institutional Landscape**
- What institutions are present in this community?
- How effective are these institutions?
- What barriers do community members face in accessing institutional services?

**Topic 3: Development Priorities**
- What are the most urgent development needs in this community?

- What development interventions have been least effective? Why?

**Topic 4: Equity and Inclusion**
- How are capital endowments distributed across different social groups?
- What barriers do marginalised groups face?
- What measures have been taken to promote inclusion?

## 6. Relationship to Existing Questionnaires

The CCDP questionnaires are designed to complement, not replace, existing national survey instruments. They draw on established questionnaires while extending them to measure the five capital dimensions of the CCDP framework.

| CCDP Questionnaire | Related Existing Instrument | Relationship |
|-------------------|---------------------------|--------------|
| CHCS Section B (Financial) | NSSO Consumer Expenditure Questionnaire | Adapted from NSSO MPCE methodology |
| CHCS Section C (Physical) | NFHS Asset Ownership Module | Directly adapted from NFHS-5 asset questions |
| CHCS Section D (Human) | NFHS Health/Education Modules + ASER Learning Assessment | Combined adaptation of NFHS and ASER instruments |
| CHCS Section E (Social) | World Bank Social Capital Assessment Tool (SCAT) | Adapted from SCAT structural and cognitive dimensions |
| CHCS Section F (Institutional) | Adapted from NFHS institutional access indicators | Extended with additional institutional engagement questions |
| CAIQ | PRA resource mapping and institutional mapping tools | Integrates established PRA tools |
| IAAM | Adapted from NFHS documentation and service access indicators | Extended with legal recognition and civic engagement questions |
| QCIS | Semi-structured interview guides from qualitative research tradition | Original development based on PRA and participatory research methods |

The CCDP questionnaires are not official government instruments. They are original frameworks developed for the CCDP and should be adapted to local contexts before deployment.

## 7. Limitations and Cautions

The questionnaires presented in this section are original frameworks developed for the Community Capital Development Project. They have not been independently validated. Users should understand the following limitations:

- **Not officially validated:** The questionnaires have not undergone formal psychometric validation. They should be pilot-tested in the target context before full deployment.
- **Context dependence:** The questionnaires are calibrated for the Indian context. They may need significant modification for other South Asian countries or for different regional settings within India.
- **Social desirability bias:** Respondents may provide socially desirable answers, particularly on questions related to income, asset ownership, and institutional access. Enumerator training should emphasise neutrality and confidentiality.
- **Enumerator bias:** Enumerator characteristics (gender, caste, education) may influence responses. Training should address these biases and strategies for mitigation.
- **Length and burden:** The Core Household Capital Survey takes 60-80 minutes to complete, which may be burdensome for respondents. Consider administering modules separately or using the Rapid Assessment Protocol for time-constrained situations.
- **Qualitative data analysis:** The Qualitative Companion Interview Schedule generates rich qualitative data that requires skilled analysis. Ensure that enumerators or analysts have training in qualitative methods.

The questionnaires presented in this section operationalise the Community Capital Development Project framework into detailed survey instruments for field deployment. They combine structured questions (for quantitative analysis) with semi-structured questions (for qualitative insight) to provide a comprehensive assessment of household and community capital endowments.

The questionnaires are designed to be flexible, adaptable, and context-sensitive. They should be pilot-tested, refined, and validated in the target context before full deployment.
