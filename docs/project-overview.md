# Project Overview

This page outlines the scope, objectives, and methodological approach of the Community Capital Development Project.

## Scope

The Community Capital Development Project examines the relationship between household-level capital endowments and broader patterns of community development. It operates at the intersection of:

- Development economics and political economy
- Household livelihood frameworks
- Class analysis and stratification studies
- Participatory development methodology
- Mixed-methods field research

## Objectives

The project pursues the following objectives:

1. **Develop original analytical frameworks** for understanding household capital formations beyond conventional income-based classifications.
2. **Classify household types** according to their composite capital endowments across economic, social, cultural, and institutional dimensions.
3. **Generate comparative analyses** of different household categories and their developmental trajectories.
4. **Construct assessment instruments** for field practitioners and policy makers to evaluate household capital positions.
5. **Formulate evidence-based policy recommendations** grounded in household capital analysis.

## Methodological Approach

The project employs a mixed-methods research design combining:

- **Qualitative analysis** of household livelihood strategies, social networks, and institutional engagement
- **Quantitative assessment** of asset endowments, income diversification, and capital accumulation patterns
- **Comparative framework analysis** across household categories and community contexts
- **Iterative framework development** through successive rounds of field testing and revision

## How to Use This Publication

This publication is designed for multiple audiences:

- **Researchers and academics** seeking frameworks for household-level capital analysis
- **Development practitioners** requiring assessment tools for community work
- **Policy makers** looking for evidence-based guidance on household-class interventions
- **Students** studying development economics and class structure analysis
